## Cameras

There are broadly three kinds of cameras you can use to stream live cams:

* A static or bullet camera - This is a non-moving camera with a fixed angle that is set when it's installed.
* A PTZ camera - This is a camera that can be controlled remotely to point & zoom. They are often more expensive than static cameras of the same quality.
* A phone, computer or portable camera - While not suited for permanent installations, a device with a camera & WiFi is a great way to get streams up and running quickly.

## Network & connections

For permanent installations, it's important that your camera be designed to be connected to a network and that it can serve a video stream reliably. Usually that means looking out for a handful of key specs:

* A wired connection, usually an ethernet cable, to connect it to your network.
* Power-over-ethernet or PoE is also how most cameras are powered. PoE specifications are little bit confusing so always double check the requirements of the cameras. This is often done through network switches that provide power or through PoE injectors, which converts a power socket to PoE.
* The camera should have built in software to connect the video stream from the camera to streaming software. Usually this would be done through a url that looks like rtsp:// or srt://. When looking through cameras, you should ensure that it supports the RTSP or SRT protocols.
* It's also important to have a camera designed for 24/7 operation. The electrical components inside of cameras have to process a lot of data and can get really hot. It's important to find cameras that are designed to handle filming all the time. Security cameras are often the best place to look for longevity.
